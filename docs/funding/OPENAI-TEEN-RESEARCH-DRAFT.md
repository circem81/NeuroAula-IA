## 1. Preliminary research concept

**Working title:** Supporting adolescent autonomy with teacher-supervised educational AI: a feasibility study of help-seeking, confidence and reliance on AI.

**Applicant / principal investigator:** [Full name, affiliation if applicable, and relevant experience — to be confirmed]

**Research setting and partners:** [Country, participating schools or research partners, and evidence of access — to be confirmed]

### Research question

How does teacher-supervised generative AI support influence adolescents’ help-seeking, confidence in their own reasoning, and ability to complete a subsequent task without AI assistance?

We would investigate both potential benefits and risks, including whether support encourages independent reasoning or increases reliance on AI-generated suggestions. We would also examine how students and teachers experience the supervision process.

### Proposed approach

We propose a staged mixed-methods feasibility study with participants aged 13–17. The initial stage would involve teachers and, following appropriate ethical approval and consent procedures, adolescents in reviewing the clarity and acceptability of the activity and interface.

A subsequent pilot would compare pre-authored educational support with AI-generated support reviewed by a teacher before being shown to a participant. To isolate the contribution of AI-generated content, both conditions would use comparable tasks, presentation, review procedures and opportunities to request help. Allocation, sample size and analysis would be defined with a qualified research partner before recruitment. This pilot would estimate feasibility and uncertainty; it would not be presented as definitive evidence of effectiveness.

Candidate measures include explanations of solution procedures, performance on a subsequent unaided task, help-request patterns, and participant-reported confidence and perceived autonomy. Appropriate age-sensitive measures would be selected with the research team. Interviews would explore students’ experiences and teachers’ workload. Cognitive traits, diagnoses and emotional states would not be inferred from activity logs.

### Role and current readiness of NeuroAula

NeuroAula is a development-stage educational prototype. Its local implementation includes a hint tutor and eight specialist workflows, with role-specific structured drafts and teacher approval or rejection. Current trials use a single authored mathematics exercise and fictional responses. The expanded implementation has passed local software tests; this does not establish pedagogical validity or readiness for research with minors.

The OpenAI integration has not yet been validated through live API use. The public Lovable site does not yet include the latest local workflows. The proposed study would start with the tutor and selected teacher-facing functions relevant to the research question, rather than activating every specialist as a separate experimental intervention.

### Safeguards and independence

Before involving minors, the team would obtain the applicable ethics review, establish informed consent and age-appropriate assent procedures, and prepare a safeguarding and incident-response protocol with suitably experienced partners. Data collection would be minimized and governed by a documented access, retention and deletion plan. These arrangements and institutional partnerships are not yet secured.

Methods and analysis would be specified in advance. Findings would be reported whether favourable, unfavourable or inconclusive. The applicant’s role in developing NeuroAula and any commercial interests would be disclosed, with independent methodological input sought to reduce conflicts of interest.

### Outputs, costs and feasibility

Proposed outputs are a feasibility report, estimates with uncertainty, qualitative findings, and practical recommendations for age-appropriate educational AI and teacher oversight. Timing would be agreed around ethics approval and recruitment, including the programme’s requested progress update in the first quarter of 2027.

A justified budget remains to be developed. Candidate categories are research personnel, methodological and safeguarding expertise, participant involvement, analysis, secure hosting, and API use if eligible. No funding amount or free API access is assumed.

Key dependencies are applicant eligibility, qualified partners, school access, ethics approval, live technical validation, and a recruitment and measurement plan. The prototype is a research tool under development, not evidence that its proposed benefits already exist.
